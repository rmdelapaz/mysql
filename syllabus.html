🗄️ MySQL Foundations: Course Syllabus

An instructor-led course in 14 three-hour sessions, in two parts. Install MySQL, build and query a real database, join and summarize its tables, then go beyond the basics (window functions, indexes, design, transactions, security and backups) and finish with a database you design yourself. Part 1 (7 sessions) also runs on its own.

📚 Course Description

Every website that remembers you keeps its data in a database, and most of them speak SQL. This course teaches MySQL 9.7 LTS from the first install to a database you design, build and defend yourself: creating tables with the right types and rules, asking precise questions with SELECT, connecting tables with keys and joins, and then the tools working developers and analysts use every week.

Every session uses the same database: an online bookstore with real classic books and fictional customers, orders and staff. It grows with you, from three empty tables in Session 2 to a relational database with orders, order items and an employee hierarchy, so every new idea is practiced on data you already know.

🎯 By the end you will be able to

  • Install MySQL 9.7, secure it, and diagnose connection errors by their numbers
  • Create databases and tables with fitting data types and constraints, and insert, update and delete rows safely
  • Filter, sort, page, summarize and nest queries, including GROUP BY and subqueries
  • Design related tables with primary and foreign keys, and combine them with joins
  • Write CTEs, recursive queries and window functions for rankings and running totals
  • Speed up queries with indexes, simplify them with views, and normalize a design
  • Use stored procedures, transactions, JSON columns, accounts and permissions, imports, exports and backups
  • Design, build and present a complete database of your own

Part 1 only: Sessions 1 to 7 (21 class hours, 13 lessons) run as a complete short course: from installing MySQL to joining tables, ending with the Part 1 assessment. Part 2 (Sessions 8 to 14) can follow later, or run on its own for learners who already have the Part 1 skills.

✅ Prerequisites

  • No database or programming experience. Comfortable with files and folders, and with a spreadsheet (filtering, sorting, a simple formula).
  • A Windows, macOS or Linux laptop on which you can install software (or run Docker), with about 1.5 GB free. MySQL 9.7 is installed at home before Session 1, following Lesson 1.2.
  • Starting with Part 2 only: Part 1 or equivalent experience (SELECT with WHERE, ORDER BY and GROUP BY, primary and foreign keys, joins, and the course's bookstore database).

💡 Format

14 sessions × 3 hours = 42 class hours, two lessons per session (Sessions 7 and 13 teach one lesson and give the rest of the time to an assessment or the final-project studio), in two parts: Part 1 (Sessions 1 to 7) and Part 2 (Sessions 8 to 14). Every lesson on the course site is written for a solo learner and takes about 75 to 110 minutes, so two lessons do not fit whole in one room session. The room protects what is hardest alone: the mental model, a live demo you type along with, a guided lab with help on hand, and starting each lesson project. At home you finish the projects and do each lesson's exercises, quiz and journal entry on the free course site: about two to three hours between sessions.

Each session runs 0:00 to 3:00: a warm-up on last session, the first lesson, a 10-minute break, the second lesson, and a wrap-up with an exit ticket and the next bring list. Single-lesson sessions (7, 13, 14) give the second half to the Part 1 assessment or the final-project studio.

Part 1 only: Sessions 1 to 7 (21 class hours, 13 lessons) run as a complete short course: from installing MySQL to joining tables, ending with the Part 1 assessment. Part 2 (Sessions 8 to 14) can follow later, or run on its own for learners who already have the Part 1 skills.

Twice weekly: about 7 weeks for the full course (Part 1 alone: 4 weeks). Two homework sets a week, so plan on four to six hours outside class.
Weekly: 14 weeks (Part 1 alone: 7), with a full week for each homework set.
Intensive days: two sessions a day; homework becomes evening practice and the final project is finished afterward.

🏁 Checkpoints, Assessment and Project

  • Self-checks and exit tickets in every session are practice, not grades: they tell you and your instructor what to revisit.
  • Checkpoint 1 (Session 4) and Checkpoint 2 (Session 11), 30 minutes each: short individual tasks on the modules just finished, scored with a short rubric.
  • Part 1 assessment (Session 7): queries and a small design task on the bookstore database, scored with a rubric. When Part 1 runs on its own, it is the course's final assessment.
  • Final project (Sessions 12 to 14). From a short brief (a neighborhood tool library), you design the tables, keys and rules, build and load the database, write the queries and views it needs, give an application account only the permissions it needs, back it up, and present it. A well-reasoned modest design scores as well as an elaborate one.

🧰 Required Software and Data

  • MySQL 9.7 LTS: Community Server: Windows MSI, macOS Homebrew or DMG, Linux APT/Yum repository, or Docker mysql:9.7 (MySQL 8.4 and MariaDB work with noted differences)
  • The mysql command-line client: installed with the server; optional graphical tools such as phpMyAdmin or MySQL Workbench
  • A text editor: for .sql files (VS Code is free and works well)
  • The course database: bookstore.sql from the course site (built step by step in Part 1, reloaded from Lesson 4.2 on)
  • No accounts or paid tools: everything runs on an ordinary laptop

MySQL installs once, at home, before Session 1, following Lesson 1.2. Your instructor sends a setup guide and an environment check a week ahead; Session 1 has an install clinic for anything that went wrong.
