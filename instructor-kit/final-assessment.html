Part 1 Assessment: The Riverside Cooking School

The assessment that closes Part 1 (Sessions 1 to 7), run in the second half of Session 7. Each learner works alone for 60 minutes, open book, in one SQL file on a database they have never seen: a fictional cooking school with instructors, classes and students. They build and protect the table that links students to classes, change data safely, and answer eight questions with filters, aggregates, subqueries and joins. Score it with the five-row rubric below. The instructor answer notes hold the reference output and what the common wrong answers print: this section is for instructors only.

Scoring and the result
  • Score each row 1 to 4 (whole numbers). Report the total out of 20 and, if your program uses percentages, the weighted score (weights in the rubric: 20 / 15 / 25 / 25 / 15).
  • Part 1 met: a total of 14 or more and no row at 1. That is Proficient on most rows, with room for one Developing row.
  • Met after revision: a total of 11 to 13, or one row at 1 with a total of 14 or more. The learner fixes the weakest row's tasks in the same file within one week; re-score those rows only.
  • Not yet: a total of 10 or less, or two rows at 1. Return written feedback (one strength per row, the single change that would help most) and offer the take-home variant once, within two weeks.
  • Run before you read: SOURCE the file first. A file that stops with an error is scored on what it printed and on the code you can read; the Script quality row takes the hit, not the content rows twice.

Part 1 Assessment: Instructor Answer Notes

A sound solution: steps
  • A1: a composite primary key, two named foreign keys (the reference: students RESTRICT, a paid booking is history; classes CASCADE, a cancelled class's bookings mean nothing without it), a CHECK, enrolled_on DATE NOT NULL, rating TINYINT nullable.
  • A2: turn on the INSERT; A3a: run the two statements at the prompt and copy the errors into comments; A3b: UPDATE classes SET price = price + 5.00 WHERE cuisine = 'Italian';; A3c: DELETE FROM classes WHERE id = 8;.
  • B1 to B4: WHERE, ORDER BY, GROUP BY, HAVING, COUNT(column) versus COUNT(*). B5: a scalar subquery. B6: four tables joined on their keys. B7: the anti-join. B8: LEFT JOIN from instructors to classes to enrollments, COUNT of an enrollment column, a sum that adds a price only for real enrollments.

Traps (and what they print)
  • Part A3 skipped, B1 run anyway: Fresh Pasta by Hand (65.00, not yet raised) joins the March list: three rows instead of two. Score B1 as right if the learner's own A3b is missing (score the missing A3b in its row), not twice.
  • B8 with SUM(c.price) and class 8 not deleted: Diego Morales shows 180.00 instead of 100.00 (the empty class's 80.00 is summed once from its NULL-padded row) and Marco Rossi 305.00 (prices not raised). With A3 done, SUM(c.price) happens to print the reference numbers: accept it as Proficient, and award Exemplary only for a sum that would stay right with an empty class.
  • B8 with inner joins: four instructors; Nadia Haddad (no classes) is missing. Developing on the joins row.
  • B4 with AVG(COALESCE(rating, 0)): blanks count as zero stars: Fresh Pasta by Hand 3.0 instead of 4.5, Sourdough Starter and French Pastry 2.5 instead of 5.0. Developing on the aggregates row.
  • B7 with an inner join and IS NULL: Empty set. An inner join has no unmatched rows.
  • B5 with the average typed in (WHERE price > 61.43): the same three classes today (the average after Part A is 61.43), wrong after the next price change. Developing on the joins/subqueries row, and note it under Script quality.
  • B3 or B6 with ambiguous columns: error 1052 stops the file at that query; the rest still runs if the learner turned it off. Script quality takes it.
What an honest result sounds like
  • A strong file reads like the reference: every answer under its TODO, a comment at A1's rules ("RESTRICT: a booking is a record of payment"), B8 with Nadia at 0.00.
  • Follow-up questions for the feedback or a short conversation: "Why can a student enroll in a class only once in your table?" · "What would B8 print if a class had no enrollments?" · "Which of your queries would break if a price changed tomorrow?"
  • A file with Part A complete and five of eight Part B answers right usually lands at 14 or 15: met. Part B answers that are wrong only because Part A was skipped are not penalized twice.

Final Project: The Maple Street Tool Library

Lesson 8.2 (lesson_final_project.html, 240 minutes for a solo learner). Brief handed out at the end of Session 12; Milestones 1 to 3 in the Session 13 studio; Milestone 4 at home; Milestones 5 to 7 and the showcase in Session 14; Milestone 8 and the README at home; due one week after Session 14.

The brief (Rosa's words, from the lesson)

Rosa Delgado coordinates the Maple Street Tool Library, a volunteer-run project that lends tools to its members. Her spreadsheet is falling apart: two people borrowed the same ladder on the same day, nobody knows who has the drill, and a member who never took the chainsaw course went home with the chainsaw. What the library needs:

  1. "We have members: name, email (one account per email), the date they joined, and whether they are active or suspended."
  2. "We own about a dozen tools. Each has a name, one category (hand tools, power tools, garden...), a condition (good, fair, or out for repair), what it would cost to replace, and the date we got it."
  3. "Some tools need a safety course first: power tools need Power Tools Basics, the chainsaw needs Chainsaw Safety. We need to know which members finished which course, and when."
  4. "A loan is one tool to one member: the day it went out, the day it is due (normally a week later) and the day it came back."
  5. "Rules: a tool can only be out with one person at a time; nothing goes out while it is being repaired; suspended members cannot borrow; nobody may have more than three tools at once; and no course, no chainsaw."
  6. "Every Saturday I need: what is on the shelf, what is overdue and whose email to write to, which tools are popular, and how busy each month was."
  7. "The volunteers at the desk check tools out and in through our little web form. Our treasurer only reads reports. Nobody should be able to delete the loan history."
  8. "If the laptop dies, I do not want to start over."

What learners hand in (lesson):

  • 01_schema.sql: creates the database and every table, and can be run again from scratch
  • 02_data.sql: realistic test data that exercises every rule
  • 03_queries.sql: Rosa's Saturday questions, one commented query each
  • 04_logic.sql: a view and the check-out and return procedures
  • 05_security.sql: roles and accounts
  • A tested backup file and README.md: a one-page write-up of your design, how to rebuild it and what you would do next
Own briefs
  • The lesson's FAQ allows another business if it has at least five tables, a many-to-many relationship, a rule that spans rows and two kinds of users. A ready-made alternative is on the page: a small physiotherapy clinic (patients, therapists, treatment types, appointments; a therapist cannot be booked twice at the same start time; cancelled appointments stay in the history; the front desk books, therapists only read). All data fictional.
  • Approve own briefs in writing in Session 13. The rubric applies unchanged; the reference numbers below do not, so the learner's README must state their own expected results (the hand checks) and you check those.
