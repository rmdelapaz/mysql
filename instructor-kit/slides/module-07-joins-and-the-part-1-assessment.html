4.3

Joins

Reading across the relationships in one query.

4.3 · Mental model

A guest list and the RSVPs

  • JOIN: each guest paired with each of their replies; no reply, no row
  • LEFT JOIN: every guest; no reply, NULLs
  • Same lists, same ON, one more word
  • Before you count: what does one row of this join stand for?
WHY ON says how the tables connect. WHERE says which results you want.
4.3 · INNER JOIN

Books with their authors

SELECT b.title, a.name AS author
FROM books AS b
JOIN authors AS a ON b.author_id = a.id
ORDER BY b.title
LIMIT 4;
+-----------------+---------------+
| title           | author        |
+-----------------+---------------+
| 1984            | George Orwell |
| Animal Farm     | George Orwell |
| Brave New World | Aldous Huxley |
| Dune            | Frank Herbert |
+-----------------+---------------+
WHY 11 books, 11 rows (4 shown). Asimov has no book: no pair, no row.
4.3 · Aliases

Short names, and WHERE after the join

SELECT b.title, b.price, a.name AS author
FROM books AS b
JOIN authors AS a ON b.author_id = a.id
WHERE a.name = 'George Orwell'
ORDER BY b.title;
+-------------+-------+---------------+
| title       | price | author        |
+-------------+-------+---------------+
| 1984        |  9.99 | George Orwell |
| Animal Farm |  8.49 | George Orwell |
+-------------+-------+---------------+
WHY JOIN = INNER JOIN. After an alias, use only the alias.
4.3 · LEFT JOIN

Every author, books or not

SELECT a.name AS author, b.title
FROM authors AS a
LEFT JOIN books AS b ON b.author_id = a.id
WHERE a.id IN (6, 9, 10)
ORDER BY a.id, b.title;
+----------------+-------------------+
| author         | title             |
+----------------+-------------------+
| J.R.R. Tolkien | Lord of the Rings |
| J.R.R. Tolkien | The Hobbit        |
| Ray Bradbury   | Fahrenheit 451    |
| Isaac Asimov   | NULL              |
+----------------+-------------------+
WHY Asimov stays, with NULL. The left table is the one before JOIN.
4.3 · Anti-join

Customers who never ordered

SELECT c.first_name, c.last_name
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.id
WHERE o.id IS NULL
ORDER BY c.id;
+------------+-----------+
| first_name | last_name |
+------------+-----------+
| Carol      | Williams  |
| Hank       | Taylor    |
+------------+-----------+
WHY LEFT JOIN ... WHERE right.id IS NULL: rows with no match.
4.3 · No ON

A join without ON is a cross join

SELECT COUNT(*) AS rows_returned
FROM books
JOIN authors;
+---------------+
| rows_returned |
+---------------+
|           110 |
+---------------+
WATCH 11 × 10. Too many rows? Check the ON first.
4.3 · Self-join

One table, two roles

SELECT e.first_name AS employee, m.first_name AS manager
FROM employees AS e
LEFT JOIN employees AS m ON e.manager_id = m.id
WHERE e.id <= 4
ORDER BY e.id;
+----------+---------+
| employee | manager |
+----------+---------+
| Maria    | NULL    |
| Daniel   | Maria   |
| Priya    | Daniel  |
| Tom      | Priya   |
+----------+---------+
WHY e = employee, m = manager. LEFT keeps Maria, who has no manager.
4.3 · Four tables

Who bought what in order 1

SELECT o.id AS order_id, b.title, oi.quantity
FROM orders AS o
JOIN customers AS c    ON o.customer_id = c.id
JOIN order_items AS oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
JOIN books AS b        ON oi.book_id = b.id
WHERE o.id = 1
ORDER BY b.title;
+----------+-------+----------+
| order_id | title | quantity |
+----------+-------+----------+
|        1 | 1984  |        2 |
|        1 | Dune  |        1 |
+----------+-------+----------+
WHY One row per order LINE: order 1 appears twice.
4.3 · GROUP BY

What each customer spent (top 2)

SELECT CONCAT(c.first_name, ' ', c.last_name) AS customer, COUNT(DISTINCT o.id) AS orders,
       COALESCE(SUM(oi.quantity * oi.price_at_time), 0) AS spent
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o       ON o.customer_id = c.id
LEFT JOIN order_items AS oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
GROUP BY c.id, c.first_name, c.last_name
ORDER BY spent DESC, customer LIMIT 2;
+---------------+--------+-------+
| customer      | orders | spent |
+---------------+--------+-------+
| Alice Johnson |      3 | 58.95 |
| Dave Brown    |      2 | 45.96 |
+---------------+--------+-------+
WHY COUNT(DISTINCT o.id) counts orders, not lines; COALESCE gives 0, not NULL.
4.3 · Trap

WHERE on the right table

SELECT c.first_name, o.order_date
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.id
WHERE o.order_date >= '2026-05-01'
ORDER BY c.first_name;
+------------+------------+
| first_name | order_date |
+------------+------------+
| Dave       | 2026-06-07 |
| Frank      | 2026-05-02 |
| Grace      | 2026-05-19 |
+------------+------------+
SILENT Three customers, not eight: the LEFT JOIN became an inner join.
4.3 · Fix

The condition goes in ON

SELECT c.first_name, o.order_date
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o
       ON o.customer_id = c.id
      AND o.order_date >= '2026-05-01'
ORDER BY c.first_name;
+------------+------------+
| first_name | order_date |
+------------+------------+
| Alice      | NULL       |
| Bob        | NULL       |
| Carol      | NULL       |
| Dave       | 2026-06-07 |
| Eve        | NULL       |
| Frank      | 2026-05-02 |
| Grace      | 2026-05-19 |
| Hank       | NULL       |
+------------+------------+
WHY Eight customers; the date decides which orders match.
4.3 · Trap

COUNT(*) after a LEFT JOIN

SELECT a.name, COUNT(*) AS books
FROM authors AS a
LEFT JOIN books AS b ON b.author_id = a.id
WHERE a.id IN (2, 10)
GROUP BY a.id, a.name
ORDER BY a.id;
+---------------+-------+
| name          | books |
+---------------+-------+
| George Orwell |     2 |
| Isaac Asimov  |     1 |
+---------------+-------+
SILENT Asimov has no books. Count the right table: COUNT(b.id).
4.3 · Errors

Three numbers to recognize

  • 1052 id is ambiguous: qualify it (o.id)
  • 1054 books.title after books AS b: use the alias
  • 1066 a table joined to itself without aliases
  • Silent: too many rows (no ON), too few (WHERE after LEFT), counts too high (fan-out)
4.3 · Predict, then run

What does this print?

SELECT c.first_name, COUNT(*) AS n
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.id
WHERE c.id IN (3, 6)
GROUP BY c.id, c.first_name
ORDER BY c.id;
  • Carol has no orders; Frank has two
  • COUNT(*) or COUNT(o.id)?
